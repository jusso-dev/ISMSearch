# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Security practitioners, CISOs, compliance leads, cloud engineers and technical reviewers who need to find, compare and interpret Australian Information Security Manual controls quickly. They are usually in an assessment, architecture review, audit preparation or implementation planning workflow, and they need answers they can trace back to official ISM source material.

## Product Purpose

ISM Search is a local-first workbench for searching ASD ISM controls, comparing releases and asking natural-language questions over the control catalogue. Success means a user can move from a plain-English concern, such as software security or privileged access, to relevant controls, release history and defensible source context without leaving the tool.

## Brand Personality

Precise, composed and technically credible. The product should feel like a serious analyst surface: fast, calm, structured and trustworthy, with enough polish to read as a strong portfolio project without compromising utility.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic SaaS dashboards, soft marketing gradients, government portal blandness and cyber-neon hacker aesthetics. Do not make the interface feel theatrical, sales-led or detached from the practical work of finding controls.

## Design Principles

1. Retrieval before decoration: every visual decision should help users find, filter, compare or cite controls faster.
2. Show provenance: answers and summaries must keep official release, version and control identifiers visible.
3. Local-first confidence: Docker, Meilisearch and Ollama should feel like a deliberate self-contained system, not a demo dependency chain.
4. Dense but legible: support expert workflows without burying important control text or release changes.
5. Portfolio through craft: make the engineering depth obvious through coherent flows, sharp states and reliable interactions.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA contrast, visible keyboard focus, semantic controls and reduced-motion-safe transitions. Use plain English labels for controls and filters, and avoid relying on color alone for classification, maturity or comparison states.
